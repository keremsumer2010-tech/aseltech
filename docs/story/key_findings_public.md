# Key Findings — What Satellite Radar Shows

> **Calibration Notice:** NISAR data are provisional. Calibration and validation are carried out by the NISAR project (NASA-JPL and ISRO), not by our team.

1. **California farming valley sank up to 7.8 cm (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Parts of the southern San Joaquin Valley sank up to 7.8 cm (range 6.2–7.8 cm depending on reference site, ±0.29 cm sensor noise uncertainty; about 10.1 cm vertical estimate, or 26.4 cm/yr summer-rate estimate), with 239.5 km² dropping more than 5 cm (`data/central_valley/summary.json`, `data/central_valley/area_stats.json`, `data/central_valley/uncertainty/meta.json`).
2. **Central Türkiye farmland sank up to 5.9 cm (26 Jun–12 Oct 2026):** Agricultural plains near Karapınar sank up to 5.9 cm (±0.33 cm sensor noise uncertainty; about 8.0 cm vertical estimate, or 20.0 cm/yr summer-rate estimate), affecting 28.7 km² by more than 2 cm and 3.1 km² by more than 5 cm (`data/karapinar/summary.json`, `data/karapinar/area_stats.json`, `data/karapinar/uncertainty/meta.json`).
3. **Ground sinking exceeds radar sensor noise by 18× (26 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Median random radar measurement noise stays near ±0.29 cm in California (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026) and ±0.33 cm in Karapınar (26 Jun–12 Oct 2026), well below the 5.9–7.8 cm ground drop (`data/central_valley/uncertainty/meta.json`, `data/karapinar/uncertainty/meta.json`).
4. **Independent ground GPS stations confirm satellite tracking (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Across 10 continuous GPS stations in the Central Valley, satellite measurements agree within a 1.00 cm median average error overall and 0.75–1.17 cm at the four fastest-sinking basin stations (`data/central_valley/validation.json`).
5. **A second satellite path sees the same sinking bowl (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Across 6,719 shared 1 km ground patches measured from a separate satellite orbit, both passes capture matching valley subsidence (2nd-percentile peak -6.47 cm vs. -7.13 cm vertical estimate) (`analysis/out/cv_p34_vs_p114.json`).
6. **Canals, highways, and rail lines cross steep sinking slopes (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Out of 645 Central Valley infrastructure segments surveyed, 81 segments (12.6%) lie in high-risk zones, led by the Tulare Irrigation District Canal tilting at 57.01 mm/km beside a 6.75 cm sinking pocket (`data/central_valley/assets.json`).
7. **Karapınar sinking clusters around active sinkholes (26 Jun–12 Oct 2026):** Terrain within 0–1 km of 29 mapped sinkholes dropped by an average of 4.15 cm—more than 3.4 cm faster than background land beyond 5 km (-0.65 cm average)—placing 10 nearby road and canal segments in the high-risk tier (`data/karapinar/assets.json`).
8. **Summer irrigation peaks right before land sinks fastest (28 Jun–14 Oct 2026):** Eight satellite snapshots over the Central Valley show peak July–August surface irrigation moisture (up to 0.184 m³/m³) followed 12 days later by accelerating ground sinking down to -7.8 cm (`data/central_valley/drivers.json`).
