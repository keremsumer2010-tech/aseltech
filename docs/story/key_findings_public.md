# Key Findings — What Satellite Radar Shows

> **Calibration Notice:** NISAR data are provisional. Calibration and validation are carried out by the NISAR project (NASA-JPL and ISRO), not by our team.

1. **California's Farmland Subsided by Up to 7.8 cm:** Parts of California's southern San Joaquin Valley experienced up to 7.8 cm of land subsidence (6.2–7.8 cm, depending on the reference site). With a sensor uncertainty of ±0.29 cm, this corresponds to an estimated vertical displacement of approximately 10.1 cm, or a summer subsidence rate of 26.4 cm/year. In total, 239.5 km² of land subsided by more than 5 cm. (`data/central_valley/summary.json`, `data/central_valley/area_stats.json`, `data/central_valley/uncertainty/meta.json`)
2. **Central Türkiye Recorded Up to 5.9 cm of Land Subsidence:** Agricultural plains near Karapınar experienced up to 5.9 cm of subsidence (±0.33 cm sensor uncertainty). The estimated vertical displacement was approximately 8.0 cm, equivalent to a summer subsidence rate of 20.0 cm/year. The affected area included 28.7 km² of land with more than 2 cm of subsidence and 3.1 km² with more than 5 cm. (`data/karapinar/summary.json`, `data/karapinar/area_stats.json`, `data/karapinar/uncertainty/meta.json`)
3. **Measured Ground Sinking Is 18 Times Greater Than Radar Noise:** Median radar measurement noise remained close to ±0.29 cm in California (June 28–October 14, 2026) and ±0.33 cm in Karapınar (June 26–October 12, 2026). These values are substantially smaller than the observed ground displacement of 5.9–7.8 cm. (`data/central_valley/uncertainty/meta.json`, `data/karapinar/uncertainty/meta.json`)
4. **Independent Ground-Based GPS Stations Confirm Satellite Measurements:** Measurements from 10 continuously operating GPS stations across California's Central Valley independently validate the satellite observations. The median overall difference was just 1.00 cm, ranging from 0.75 to 1.17 cm at the four fastest-subsiding basin stations. (`data/central_valley/validation.json`)
5. **A Second Satellite Pass Confirms the Same Subsidence Pattern:** When a second satellite checked the exact same 6,719 land areas, it found the exact same sinking pattern. The deepest sinking measurements reached peaks of −6.47 cm and −7.13 cm, respectively, proving that the data is reliable. (`analysis/out/cv_p34_vs_p114.json`)
6. **Critical Infrastructure Crosses Areas of Rapid Land Subsidence:** Of the 645 infrastructure segments surveyed across California's Central Valley, 81 (12.6%) were located in high-risk zones. One particularly notable example is the Tulare Irrigation District Canal, which experiences a tilt of 57.01 mm/km alongside a 6.75 cm subsidence pocket. (`data/central_valley/assets.json`)
7. **Land Subsidence Intensifies Around Active Sinkholes in Karapınar:** Land right next to sinkholes sank by an average of 4.15 cm—much faster than normal land far away, which only changed by −0.65 cm. Because the ground is sinking so unevenly in these areas, 10 nearby roads and canals are now considered high-risk. (`data/karapinar/assets.json`)
8. **Peak Summer Irrigation Precedes the Fastest Subsidence:** Eight satellite observations across California's Central Valley revealed peak surface irrigation moisture in July and August (up to 0.184 m³/m³). Twelve days later, accelerating subsidence reached −7.8 cm. (`data/central_valley/drivers.json`)
