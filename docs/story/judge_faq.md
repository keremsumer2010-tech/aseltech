### 1. Are your NISAR measurements fully calibrated science products?
**No—every science layer in our viewer is transparently labeled `quality: provisional` (`CRID P05023`, acquired on or after `2026-06-17`).** *NISAR data are provisional. Calibration and validation are carried out by the NISAR project (NASA-JPL and ISRO), not by our team.* Pre-calibration `beta` granules prior to `2026-06-17` are excluded from quantitative cumulative charts, and the UK Fens optical-vs-radar pair is labeled `preview`.

### 2. Why show Line-of-Sight (LOS) displacement instead of calling it pure vertical sinking?
**InSAR directly measures one-dimensional motion along the radar's slanted Line of Sight (`~39.1°` incidence in Central Valley, `~41.9°–42.0°` in Karapınar), not strictly vertical elevation.** We keep base raster maps honestly in LOS (`−λ / 4π × Δφ`, negative = away from satellite), project peak values to vertical estimates (`LOS / cos θ`, giving `−10.1 cm` vs. `−7.8 cm` LOS in Central Valley and `−8.0 cm` vs. `−5.9 cm` LOS in Karapınar), cross-check Track 34 ascending against Track 114 descending (`6,719` 1 km blocks: `r = 0.489` / `0.49` overall, `0.60` local `< 15 km`, `RMSE = 2.65 cm`, `median diff = −1.29 cm`, `85%` sign agreement where `|d| > 3 cm`, `−5.7 cm` Track 114 median where Track 34 `< −6 cm`), and solve a **`Q22` two-orbit pixel decomposition** (`data/central_valley/vertical.json`: Track 34 `e = +0.64, u = 0.747` + Track 114 `e = −0.60, u = 0.774`), isolating **`−7.2 cm` vertical subsidence** with **`−0.1 cm` east-west motion** at the **Alpaugh / Earlimart bowl** (max `−9.8 cm` vertical block).

### 3. How did you handle ionosphere and troposphere atmospheric noise in GUNW products?
**ASF documentation notes that `GUNW unwrappedPhase` does not subtract ionosphere or troposphere corrections by default, so our pipeline applies layer-level and spatial mitigation on every pair.** Specifically, `analysis/cumulative.py` subtracts the shipped GUNW `ionospherePhaseScreen` (measuring `2.93–17.26 cm` LOS `p1–p99` dispersion per pair) and fits an elevation-aware plane per interferogram, yielding **`0.66 cm` local post-deramp single-pair RMS**, **`1.74 cm` local** (`2.87 cm` full-scene) cumulative chain uncertainty (`SNR 2.7×–4.5×` at `−7.8 cm` LOS; 3-reference spread `−7.8` to `−4.7 cm` LOS in `summary.json`), and per-pixel Cramér-Rao formal phase-noise lower bounds of median **`0.294 cm`** LOS in Central Valley (`0.326 cm` in Karapınar, `L = 208` nominal looks, `data/*/uncertainty/meta.json`).

### 4. How do you stop phase unwrapping errors or noisy agricultural pixels from skewing the headline peaks?
**Multi-stage physical masking, per-point jump QA, and a conservative spatial block statistic prevent single-pixel spikes from dominating.** Pixels require `coherence >= 0.3` and `connectedComponents != 0`, isolated fragments under `200 px` (`~1.3 km²`) and single-pair jumps deviating over `λ/4` from their `720 m` local median or `> 15.0 cm` are dropped, and headline basin maxima use `1 km` (`13×13 px`) block medians (`>= 50%` valid pixels, `std <= 2 cm`); an independent time-series audit (`docs/qa/PC5.md`) also confirmed **zero half-cycle (`>= 6.05 cm`) unwrapping jumps** across all `12` Central Valley and `12` Karapınar sampled points (`28 Jun–14 Oct 2026` and `26 Jun–12 Oct 2026`).

### 5. Do your annualized estimates (`−26.4 cm/yr` CV and `−20.0 cm/yr` Karapınar) mean those basins sink that much every year?
**No—we explicitly mark those numbers as linear extrapolations of `108` summer irrigation days (`2026-06-28 → 2026-10-14` CV; `2026-06-26 → 2026-10-12` Karapınar), not net multi-year averages.** In confined aquifer systems like the San Joaquin Valley, wet-season recharge produces partial elastic rebound in winter while agricultural water demand peaks mid-summer (`data/central_valley/drivers.json` shows `r = 0.654` with `SME2`-derived irrigation intensity and `r = 0.638` with 12-day lagged soil moisture, though exact permutation testing over `N = 8` acquisitions in `docs/story/drivers_analysis.md` yields `p_perm = 0.088` and `p_perm = 0.130`—below `α = 0.05` thresholds `|r_crit| = 0.707–0.754`, with detrended interval velocities at `r = 0.037–0.130`, `p >= 0.81`—so full-year `Q08` data `N >= 24–30` are needed to test causal lag). Multi-year published rates span `−17.7` to `−34.5 cm/yr` peak in Sentinel-1 (`Neely et al. 2021`, up to `−60 cm/yr` during `2015–2016` drought) and `−1.2` to `−5.2 cm/yr` GNSS / `> −7.0 cm/yr` InSAR across Karapınar (`Üstün et al. 2010`; `Orhan et al. 2021`, `docs/story/literature_comparison.md`), showing that summer-only scaling illustrates seasonal stress and overstates net annual compaction (`data/central_valley/summary.json`).

### 6. Why combine California, Konya–Karapınar, and the UK (plus Mexico City) into a single story?
**Together they compare four distinct human-driven subsidence settings—including an honest negative-control test and an urban benchmark—under one physical water story.** California captures deep aquifer clay compaction tilting canals; Karapınar shows carbonate karst collapse (*obruks*) linked to agricultural overdraft; Mexico City (`data/global/mexico_city/summary.json`, `8` provisional pairs `2026-06-22` to `2026-10-20`) demonstrates pipeline transferability across a lacustrine clay megacity (`−4.4 cm` 1 km block median LOS peak / `−6.2 cm` vertical estimate at `44.2°` incidence; `−7.4` to `−4.4 cm` across `2` urban references); and in the UK (`docs/story/uk_findings.md`) we contrast historical peat drainage (`Holme Fen` **~4 m** post since `1851`) and the `2026-10-20` cloud-penetrating UK Fens `GCOV` preview with a **`9`-pair provisional GUNW chain over London clay (`2026-06-21` to `2026-10-19`, `120` days, Track 101 ascending frame 28, `data/uk_clay/detection.json`)** reporting **`status: "below_detection"`** (`quality: "provisional"`): `chain_sigma = 1.21 cm`, **`3σ` detection threshold `= 3.62 cm`**, and after quadratic long-wavelength atmosphere removal **`97.7%` (`98%`) of `1 km` blocks lie within `±1.5 cm`** (`p2/p50/p98 = −1.47 / +0.19 / +1.26 cm`, `0` interior blocks exceeding `3.62 cm`; raw `-5.29 cm` at `2.0 km` from the scene boundary is an edge atmospheric artifact). This negative control shows that seasonal clay shrink–swell (`<= 1 cm`) sits below single-season atmospheric noise and prevents reporting noise as subsidence.

### 7. Can NISAR predict the exact location and date a new Karapınar sinkhole will collapse?
**No—NISAR maps regional multi-pixel ground settling, not catastrophic sub-pixel cavity roof failure days before collapse.** Still, combining our `8`-pair track-7 stack (`−5.9 cm` LOS peak `2 km W of Karapınar`) with our `29` verified historic sinkholes (`data/karapinar/assets.json`) reveals a statistically significant spatial association (`docs/story/sinkhole_stats.md`): valid pixels inside **`0–1 km` of mapped sinkholes average `−4.15 cm` LOS** (`6.4×` the `>5 km` background of `−0.65 cm`, `100%` exceeding `−2 cm`, led by `Köken Obruğu-I` at `−4.71 cm`), and across **`0–2 km` (`n = 236` pixels across `16` coherent sinkholes) average `−4.06 cm`** (`−3.41 cm` deeper than `>5 km`). Accounting for spatial autocorrelation via non-overlapping **`1.04 km` (`13×13 px`) blocks** (`14` near vs. `362` background: **`−3.04 cm` diff, permutation `p = 0.0001`**, Welch `p = 9.7 × 10⁻⁸`) and **`3,423` rigid `29`-sinkhole constellation shifts** (**`p = 0.0207`**) confirms genuine clustering over shared groundwater stress without claiming day-ahead collapse prediction.

### 8. How well do NISAR displacement measurements match independent GPS (GNSS) stations and another orbit?
**Across `10` continuous Nevada Geodetic Laboratory (`IGS20/NA`) stations inside the Central Valley frame, NISAR vertical estimates (`LOS / cos θ`) achieve `1.00 cm` median RMSE (`1.37 cm` final-epoch RMSE, `r = 0.924` cross-station correlation, `1.33 cm` mean time-series RMSE; best `0.64 cm` at `P566`), and independent dual-orbit geometry confirms true vertical bowl sinking (`docs/story/validation_report.md`).** At active stations, the `4` largest-moving sites (`|GPS| > 2.0 cm`: `TLBN, CAHA, QCAP, HANF`) achieve **`0.76 cm` final RMSE** (`r = 0.851` mean temporal), the `5` active-basin sites average **`0.86 cm` RMSE / `r = 0.81`** (`docs/story/uncertainty.md`), while **Track 34 ascending** vs. **Track 114 descending** (`6,719` 1 km blocks: `r = 0.489`, `RMSE = 2.65 cm`, `median diff = −1.29 cm`, `r = 0.60` local `< 15 km`) reach **`85%` sign agreement** (`|d| > 3 cm`) and **`−7.2 cm` vertical (`−0.1 cm` east-west)** in `Q22` two-orbit inversion at the Alpaugh bowl (`data/central_valley/vertical.json`).

### 9. How did you translate centimetre radar maps into concrete infrastructure risk?
**Flat sinking rarely breaks pipes or levees; steep differential tilting across rigid linear corridors causes structural strain.** `data/central_valley/assets.json` samples cumulative NISAR slopes (`mm/km`) along `645` OpenStreetMap canals, railways, and highways (`>= 1 km` span), flagging **`81` high-risk segments** (`12.6%`) topped by `Tulare Irrigation District Canal` (`57.01 mm/km`) and `102.8 km` of the `California Aqueduct` (`49.12 mm/km`). Moreover, `±25%` threshold sensitivity testing (`7.5 / 10.0 / 12.5 mm/km` and `cm` in `docs/story/risk_sensitivity.md`) shifts the total high-risk count between `72` (`11.2%`) and `115` (`17.8%`) while **leaving the top-10 priority segment list and its exact rank order (`42.34–57.01 mm/km`) 100% invariant**.

### 10. Why does NISAR's 24 cm L-band radar succeed over crops and clouds where earlier satellites struggle?
**Long `24.20 cm` microwaves (`12.10 cm` full `2π` fringe, `6.05 cm` half-cycle limit) penetrate thick cloud decks that blind optical missions and pass deeper through seasonal crop canopies than `~5.55–5.66 cm` C-band radars (`2.77 cm` fringe, `1.39 cm` half-cycle limit, `docs/story/cross_sensor.md`).** Over California's Tulare agricultural core, NISAR retains **`80.4%` valid pixels (`coh >= 0.30`, median `0.539`)** during peak summer irrigation (`92.7%` in winter `coh 0.662`, `77.6%` in spring `coh 0.493`) with **`RMSE = 0.823 cm` (`r = 0.7462`)** against `5` subsiding-core GPS stations (`CAHA, HANF, QCAP, TLBN, P056`), whereas the top `5` Tulare/Earlimart `12–24`-day steps (`−2.89` to `−5.10 cm`) exceed C-band's `2.77 cm` fringe and NASA's `OPERA_L3_DISP-S1_V1` C-band archive (`2,663` granules) ends on `2026-04-26` (`0` granules in `2026-06-28 → 2026-10-14`). Over eastern England on `2026-10-20`, same-day VIIRS true-color imagery is ~98% cloud-obscured while NISAR L-band `GCOV` resolves every coastal inlet around The Wash.

### 11. What do the UK Fens optical-vs-radar preview, `HH`/`HV` crop curves, and population/crop exposure maps measure?
**First, the `2026-10-20` UK Fens optical-vs-radar swipe is strictly labeled `quality: "preview"` in `data/uk/meta.json`—an uncalibrated qualitative GIBS VIIRS vs. NISAR `GCOV` (`700×500` RGB) cloud-penetration comparison, not a displacement measurement.** Second, across **`4` `480 m` Fens agricultural windows (`22 Jun – 8 Oct 2026`, `8` provisional `GCOV` acquisitions, `docs/story/polarization.md`, `data/fens/crops.json`)**, `HH` co-polarized backscatter is **`~7 dB` brighter** than `HV` with a **`4.27 dB` mean temporal range** versus **`3.49 dB` for `HV`** (`1.36 dB` for `10 log10(HV/HH)`), separating soil moisture/surface roughness (`HH`) from 3D crop canopy structure (`HV`, `SAR Handbook Ch. 2`). Third, overlaying WorldPop (`100 m`, 2020), USDA NASS CDL (`30 m`, 2021), and ESA WorldCover (`10 m`, 2021) onto our `> 2 cm` LOS footprints (`docs/story/exposure.md`) yields an *estimated* **`141,555` residents** (**`71,701` Central Valley** + **`69,854` Konya Closed Basin**, of which `11,572` sit inside the Karapınar subset; `20,229` inside `> 5 cm` cores) and **`79.2%` (`1,251.37 km²`)** of Central Valley `> 2 cm` subsiding land (`91.2%` of `> 5 cm`) in cultivated cropland led by **citrus (`355.64 km²`, `22.5%`)** and **pistachios (`230.92 km²`, `14.6%`)**.

### 12. Where did your 29 Karapınar sinkholes come from, and why not map all 104 from the literature?
**Every point in `data/karapinar/sinkholes.geojson` comes from peer-reviewed DMS coordinates in Tapur & Bozyiğit (2015), *Marmara Coğrafya Dergisi* 31 (`http 200`), filtered strictly to the Karapınar radar frame `[33.3, 37.6, 33.8, 37.9]`.** Although Konya Province totals `104` catalogued sinkholes (`86` in Karapınar), our zero-fabrication rule barred estimating missing coordinates—including skipping `Küpbasan Yeni Obruk-I` due to a `37° 43' 99"` typographical error in the source table.

### 13. How is the `Valley Under Pressure` simulator connected to your NASA radar measurements?
**The standalone physics game (`game/index.html`) calibrates directly against our measured Central Valley NISAR target in `data/central_valley/summary.json` (`−7.8 cm` LOS / `−10.1 cm` vertical est. over `108` summer days).** Its demand multiplier `k = 0.6232` reproduces `−10.10 cm` vertical (`−7.84 cm` LOS) with `0.00%` calibration residual along the equifinality contour `b0 · S_skv ≈ 0.02356`, and `N = 1,000` Monte Carlo sensitivity trials (`docs/story/game_sensitivity.md`) span `−13.69` to `−4.12 cm` (`p10–p90`) over `108` days and `−27.10` to `−6.23 cm` over `24` stochastic water-year months (`−18.69 cm` nominal `Below Normal × 2`)—allowing users to weigh elastic recovery against permanent clay collapse and canal loss (`illustrative` priors flagged in-app).

### 14. Can external researchers audit and reproduce every step of your pipeline?
**Yes—every script, intermediate contract, unit test, and documentation check lives in an open MIT-licensed GitHub repository (`keremsumer2010-tech/aseltech`).** Pure Python (`numpy`/`scipy`) routines in `analysis/insar.py` and `cumulative.py` enforce automated `pytest` verification on phase-to-cm conversion and masking, feeding zero-backend static web pages (`web/`, `web3d/`, `game/`).

### 15. How did your team use AI tools, and how did you prevent scientific hallucinations?
**The public site (`web/`), slide deck, 3D viewer, simulator, and archived `demo_30s.mp4` draft use no AI-generated images—all maps, charts, and previews render measured data (`web/assets/simulator.jpg` is a real simulator capture, and commit `38b774f` removed all 7 unused early scene illustrations).** AI coding and writing assistants (Claude, Gemini/Antigravity) helped scaffold Python processing, unit tests, and documentation under strict verification: every sinkhole coordinate was verified line-by-line against primary PDFs, every statistic reads dynamically from JSON, and all **`43/43`** unique external URLs in [`docs/story/sources.md`](sources.md) passed live `HTTP 200` checks.

### 16. Why does the 11-month Central Valley full-year chain include pre-calibration `beta` data alongside `provisional` products?
**To separate winter elastic rebound from permanent compaction across an entire annual cycle (`2025-11-24` to `2026-10-14`), we had to connect `18` pre-calibration `beta` pairs (`< 2026-06-17`) to `7` `provisional` summer pairs (`25` pairs total, `324` days, `data/central_valley_fullyear/summary.json`).** Because `beta` granules carry radiometric-banding and mask caveats, the entire series is strictly isolated in a separate `quality: "mixed"` dataset, drawn with dashed lines and explicit caution banners in the explorer, and separated from our `provisional`-only headline tables (`docs/story/fullyear.md`). Near Earlimart (`p01`), this mixed series measures `−20.3 cm` cumulative LOS (`−26.9 cm` vertical estimate) and decomposes (`R² = 0.97`) into **`−16.8 ± 2.2 cm/yr` permanent compaction** versus **`11.7 cm` peak-to-peak seasonal rebound** (`data/karapinar_fullyear/` covers `6` early pairs from `2025-10-17` to `2026-01-09` with the same `mixed` warning).

### 17. What did you find in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta levee network?
**Measuring the `NISAR Science Users' Handbook` Figure 2-4 levee target directly (`data/delta/summary.json`, `docs/story/delta.md`, `9` provisional Path 42 pairs `2026-06-17` to `2026-10-15`, `120` days) shows a broad `1 km` block-median peak of `−1.3 cm` LOS (`−1.3` to `−1.1 cm` reference range, `−1.6 cm` vertical estimate at `35.8°`), while localized levee samples highlight narrow settlement reaches.** Sampling `N = 87` OpenStreetMap levee and embankment alignments yields a `10th` percentile of `−3.48 cm` LOS and reaches `−11.36 cm` on a `4.09 km` reach; rather than treating every single-pixel extreme as confirmed levee failure, we flag those reaches for targeted piezometer and crest re-leveling checks.

### 18. How does the identical code perform across global cities and the whole Konya Closed Basin?
**Running the exact same Python pipeline unchanged (`pipeline/subset.py` + `analysis/cumulative.py`) resolves active drawdown in four sinking cities plus the full Konya Closed Basin (`docs/story/global_showcase.md`, `docs/story/konya_plain.md`), while honestly exposing reference-point sensitivity and tropical decorrelation.** Across `108–120` provisional summer days (`8–9` pairs each), `1 km` block-median LOS peaks reach **`−4.4 cm` in Mexico City** (`−7.4` to `−4.4 cm` across 2 references), **`−3.5 cm` in Tehran** (`−7.3` to `−3.5 cm`), **`−6.8 cm` in Jakarta** (1 reference only; tropical vegetation leaves only `2.9%` common valid pixels), **`−1.4 cm` in Beijing** (`−7.6` to `−1.4 cm`), and **`−9.6 cm` across the full Konya Closed Basin** (`−14.0` to `−9.6 cm` across 3 references, south of Konya city)—showing that city peaks depend strongly on where the stable reference patch sits.

### 19. How does NISAR L-band compare quantitatively with C-band Sentinel-1 (`OPERA DISP-S1`)?
**Over California's irrigated Tulare agricultural core, NISAR's `24.20 cm` L-band radar (`12.10 cm` full `2π` fringe, `6.05 cm` half-cycle limit) retains `80.4%` coherent pixels (`coh >= 0.30`, median `0.539`) in summer and tracks `5` core GPS stations at `RMSE = 0.823 cm` (`r = 0.7462`), whereas `~5.55 cm` C-band has a `2.77 cm` fringe (`1.39 cm` half-cycle limit, `docs/story/cross_sensor.md`, `data/central_valley_s1/cross_sensor.json`, `quality: "mixed"`).** Five individual `12–24`-day Tulare/Earlimart steps (`−2.89` to `−5.10 cm`) exceed C-band's `2.77 cm` ambiguity limit, and NASA CMR queries (`2,663` `OPERA_L3_DISP-S1_V1` granules) show the operational C-band archive ending on `2026-04-26` (`0` frames during our `2026-06-28 → 2026-10-14` summer window).

### 20. What are the main limitations of your population and crop exposure estimates?
**Our exposure numbers (`docs/story/exposure.md`) overlay static historical population and land-cover rasters—WorldPop (`100 m`, `2020`), USDA NASS CDL (`30 m`, `2021`), and ESA WorldCover (`10 m`, `2021`)—onto `2026` summer InSAR displacement masks, so crop rotations and urban growth since `2020–2021` are not captured.** Furthermore, counts shift substantially between strict valid-pixel masks (`141,555` residents across Central Valley + Konya Basin `> 2 cm` LOS) and `<= 2.5 km` basin-interpolated bounds (`196,949` residents) or vertical *estimates* (`203,822` residents), because agricultural decorrelation masks out flooded or tilled fields; accordingly, every exposure figure is reported as an *estimate* with both strict and interpolated bounds.

### 21. Why build a 2026 Global Subsidence Atlas with a single command, and how do you compare basins fairly?
**By running the exact same `provisional`-only (`>= 2026-06-17`, `CRID P05023`) `pipeline/nisarpulse/` workflow on standardized `±0.25°` (`~55 × 55 km`) boxes and ranking by period-independent `rate_cm_per_100d` (`cm / 100 days LOS`), we compare `108`-day and `120`-day basins on an equal footing without site-specific tuning.** Currently, Konya Basin (`−8.89 cm/100d`, pending PC5 literature check), Central Valley (`−7.22 cm/100d`, `high`), Jakarta (`−6.30 cm/100d`, `low — indicative only`, `2.9%` valid pixels), Karapınar (`−5.46 cm/100d`, `medium`), and Mexico City (`−3.67 cm/100d`, scheduled for outside-basin reference re-run) lead the seed ranking; every entry carries a `high / medium / low` confidence badge, requires an outside-basin stable reference window, and separates low-coverage tropical frames as `indicative only`.
