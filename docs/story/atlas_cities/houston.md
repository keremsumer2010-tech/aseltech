# Houston, USA — Gulf Coast Clay & Fault Compaction

**Driver & Risk:** Groundwater pumping and Gulf Coast clay compaction—interacting with growth faults across suburban Harris, Fort Bend, and Waller counties—lower floodplains and heighten flood exposure.

**Literature:** Liu et al. ([2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14236184)) mapped ~1 cm/yr modern peripheral subsidence via Sentinel-1/GPS, while Miller & Shirzaei ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2019.03.022)) reported pre-Harvey local rates up to 3.4–4.9 cm/yr.

**NISAR L-Band Measurement (`provisional`):** From 2026-06-18 to 2026-10-16 (9 pairs, 120 days), NISAR records **−5.2 cm** peak 1 km LOS subsidence (**−4.0 cm/100d** common-window rate; −4.3 cm/100d full, −6.7 cm vertical estimate; 46.1% valid pixels; 2-ref range −5.2 to −3.3 cm; `medium` confidence, rank #10).

**NISAR Value:** L-band penetrates humid Gulf Coast tree cover and suburban lawns to capture summer seasonal-plus-permanent clay drawdown across the metropolitan fringe.

*NISAR data are provisional. Calibration and validation are carried out by the NISAR project (NASA-JPL and ISRO), not by our team.*
