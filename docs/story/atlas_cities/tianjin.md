# Tianjin, China — Coastal Plain & Binhai Compaction

**Driver & Risk:** Multi-aquifer groundwater extraction across the North China Plain and coastal consolidation in Binhai New Area compress thick clays, straining rail corridors, port embankments, and drainage.

**Literature:** Zhao et al. ([2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26041203)) documented two decades of Tianjin subsidence via multi-temporal InSAR, with inland cones exceeding 5 cm/yr (2003–2010) and localized coastal/fault-controlled rates up to ~17 cm/yr.

**NISAR L-Band Measurement (`provisional`):** Over 2026-06-21 to 2026-10-19 (8 pairs, 120 days), NISAR records **−2.9 cm** peak 1 km LOS subsidence (**−2.8 cm/100d** common-window rate; −2.4 cm/100d full, −3.6 cm vertical estimate; 63.4% valid pixels; 2-ref range −2.9 to −1.3 cm; `medium` confidence, rank #12).

**NISAR Value:** Consistent L-band coherence across peri-urban farmland tracks Binhai and suburban sinking bowls in one summer stack.

*NISAR data are provisional. Calibration and validation are carried out by the NISAR project (NASA-JPL and ISRO), not by our team.*
