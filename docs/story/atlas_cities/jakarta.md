# Jakarta, Indonesia (`jakarta`)

**Status:** Preliminary · **Confidence:** Low (`indicative only`)

- **Why it is sinking:** Heavy groundwater extraction from compressible Holocene marine and alluvial clays, combined with urban building loads, drives rapid coastal compaction ([Abidin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-011-9866-9)).
- **Who is affected:** Coastal neighborhoods, seawalls, port facilities, and drainage networks exposed to compounding tidal and river flooding.
- **Published rate:** −1 to −15 cm/yr average (−7.3 to −7.7 cm/yr in recent Sentinel-1/GNSS studies; historical northern hotspots −20 to −28 cm/yr; [Abidin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-011-9866-9)).
- **Our NISAR measurement (preliminary, indicative only):** −6.3 cm/100d LOS (−6.8 cm LOS, −9.3 cm vertical est., 2026-06-27 to 2026-10-13, 8 provisional pairs). Only 2.9%–3.0% of pixels remain valid across all pairs due to dense tropical vegetation and urban decorrelation, and only one coastal reference window was usable.
- **What NISAR adds:** Even where tropical humidity and partial coverage limit full-chain coherence, L-band highlights localized coastal deformation pockets for targeted follow-up.
