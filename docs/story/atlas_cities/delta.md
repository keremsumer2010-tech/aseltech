# Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, USA (`delta`)

**Status:** Press-approved (PC5) · **Confidence:** High

- **Why it is sinking:** Drainage for agriculture exposes thick Holocene freshwater peat to microbial oxidation, producing steady organic soil loss and compaction ([Deverel & Leighton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.15447/sfews.2010v8iss2art5); [Deverel et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.15447/sfews.2016v14iss2art4)).
- **Who is affected:** 1,700 km of earthen levees, farmed island interiors lying up to 8 m below mean sea level, and freshwater intake hubs supplying 27 million Californians.
- **Published rate:** −1.2 to −2.2 cm/yr modern oxidation subsidence on Bacon and Sherman islands ([Deverel & Leighton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.15447/sfews.2010v8iss2art5)).
- **Our NISAR measurement:** −1.1 cm/100d LOS (−1.3 cm LOS, −1.6 cm vertical est., 2026-06-17 to 2026-10-15, 120 days; 43.4% valid pixels, ref sensitivity range [−1.3, −1.1] cm).
- **What NISAR adds:** L-band phase stability tracks organic levee settlement across vegetated tracts where shorter radar wavelengths decorrelate.
