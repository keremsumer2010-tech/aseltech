# Beijing, China (`beijing`)

**Status:** Preliminary · **Confidence:** Low (`indicative only`)

- **Why it is sinking:** Extensive groundwater extraction from multi-layer Quaternary alluvial-lacustrine aquifers drains compressible silt-clay aquitards, driving localized consolidation in the Chaoyang and Tongzhou depression funnels ([Chen et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2016.03.018); [Zhou et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.04.053)).
- **Who is affected:** Beijing-Tianjin and Beijing-Shanghai high-speed rail lines, subway tunnels, expressways, and underground utility networks.
- **Published rate:** −1.0 to −4.5 cm/yr plain average; −10 to −15 cm/yr localized peaks in eastern Chaoyang-Tongzhou funnels ([Chen et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2016.03.018)).
- **Our NISAR measurement (preliminary):** −1.17 cm/100d LOS (−1.4 cm LOS, −2.1 cm vertical est., 2026-06-17 to 2026-10-15, 120 days; 73.0% valid pixels). The primary reference fell inside Tongzhou; referencing the northwestern foothills yields −7.6 cm LOS (−11.2 cm vertical est.).
- **What NISAR adds:** High L-band urban coherence maps regional deformation gradients cutting across critical high-speed rail corridors.
