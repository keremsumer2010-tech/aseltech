## From Ankara to the Konya Closed Basin (`Konya Kapalı Havzası`)

Our team is based in **Ankara, Türkiye**, directly north of the **Konya Closed Basin**—one of Anatolia's largest grain- and sugar-beet-producing plateaus and one of its driest hydrologically closed basins. Across Central Anatolia, decades of intensive groundwater irrigation under semi-arid rainfall have drawn down deep carbonate and alluvial water tables, turning subsurface water stress into a visible surface hazard across **Konya Province** and especially **Karapınar District**.

## Sinkholes (*Obruks*) Moved from Geological Footnotes to Everyday Headlines

Across Türkiye, Karapınar's limestone sinkholes (*obruks*) have become a recurring national headline as new collapses open beside irrigated fields, rural settlements, and regional roads:
- Peer-reviewed field inventories by [Tapur & Bozyiğit (2015)](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/3454) and [Tapur & Bozyiğit (2016)](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/227372) catalog **104 documented sinkholes** across Konya Province, with **86 located in Karapınar district** and **43 modern collapses recorded between 1972 and 2014**.
- Among the **`29` field-verified sinkholes** plotted inside our NISAR study frame (`data/karapinar/sinkholes.geojson`, [`docs/story/sinkhole_timeline.md`](sinkhole_timeline.md)), **`24` collapses (`82.8%`) occurred during the concentrated `2007–2013` period**, led by deep shafts such as **Akviran Obruğu** (`80 m` deep, `24 m` diameter) and **Yarımoğlu Obruğu** (`76 m` deep, `2009`).
- In **2008**, **İnoba Obruğu** (`42 m` deep, `29 m` diameter) collapsed next to inhabited dwellings, prompting **7 families** to relocate ([Tapur & Bozyiğit 2015](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/3454)), while earlier Sentinel-1, COSMO-SkyMed, and GNSS studies measured multi-year agricultural basin sinking exceeding **`-7.0 cm/yr`** ([Orhan et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7865528/)) and **`-1.2 to -5.2 cm/yr`** ([Üstün et al. 2010](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/10/1151/2010/)).

## Connecting Central Valley, UK Fens, and Karapınar Through Open NISAR Radar

When we studied [NASA's NISAR Land Subsidence StoryMap](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/109e602e748346f3be8dc89124619828) on California's San Joaquin Valley and the California Aqueduct, we recognized the exact same hidden physical chain affecting Central Anatolia:
- In **California's Central Valley** (compared against **10 continuous GPS stations** at `1.00 cm` median RMSE, `r = 0.924`), NISAR tracks up to **`−7.8 cm` LOS** (`−10.1 cm` vertical *estimate*) over `108` summer days (`239.54 km²` sinking `> 5 cm`) and flags `81 / 645` tilting canals and roads.
- Applying that L-band method directly to our neighboring **Karapınar basin (`26 Jun – 12 Oct 2026`, `8` provisional pairs)** measures **`−5.9 cm` LOS peak subsidence** (`−8.0 cm` vertical *estimate*) `2 km W of Karapınar`, confirms statistically (`p = 0.0001` permutation test, [`sinkhole_stats.md`](sinkhole_stats.md)) that farmland within `1 km` of our `29` verified sinkholes sank **`6.4×` faster (`−4.15 cm`)** than terrain beyond `5 km` (`−0.65 cm`), and flags Highway `42-42` tilting at `19.74 mm/km` only `597 m` from **Köken Obruğu-II**.
- Paired with the **1851 Holme Fen peat benchmark** in eastern England (`~4 m` exposed, `2.75 m` below sea level, where NISAR cuts through `98%` cloud cover), **NISAR Vision** translates multi-gigabyte radar files into clear, honest groundwater intelligence for both international engineers and communities across Türkiye.
