NISAR Vision: Follow the Water

We turned the first months of NASA-ISRO NISAR radar data into a self-checking global subsidence atlas: 29 basins measured with one open pipeline on the same 93 days, every number traceable to its file, and every headline checked against GPS, NASA's OPERA product and the literature.

Four original contributions

  1. One window, one method, 29 basins. A comparable global ranking from the first provisional NISAR data: one 93-day window, one open pipeline.
  2. An atlas that checks itself. Time-series quality control caught a full phase-cycle error in Semarang (-12.1 to -6.2 cm) and flagged other basins as indicative.
  3. Independent evidence. GPS agreement in the Central Valley (r = 0.92, 10 stations); in Mexico City spatial pattern matches NASA JPL OPERA at 10 points (r = 0.91) across seasons.
  4. From measurement to decision. People and aquifer loss, canal tilt risk, one-page bulletins, a 24-month simulator, one command to reproduce.
