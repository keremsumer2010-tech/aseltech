## 1. UK Fens & Holme Fen Posts (Peatland Subsidence)

- **Holme Fen Posts — The Great Fen**  
  URL: https://www.greatfen.org.uk/about-great-fen/heritage/holme-fen-posts  
  Key finding: In **1848**, engineer John Lawrence drove a timber pile through **22 feet** of peat into the underlying clay for landowner William Wells ahead of draining Whittlesey Mere; replaced in **1851** with a cast-iron column, immediately post-drainage subsidence reached **9 inches per year**, and **approximately 4 metres** of the post sits exposed above ground today (since **1852**; braced with steel guys and paired with a second post **6 m** northeast in **1957**).

- **Holme Fen — Wikipedia**  
  URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme_Fen  
  Key finding: The **269.4-hectare (666-acre)** National Nature Reserve and biological Site of Special Scientific Interest at Holme Fen is recognized as the lowest land point in the United Kingdom at **2.75 metres (9.0 ft) below sea level**, driven by agricultural peat drainage.

- **Infrastructural and Societal Impacts of Water Level Management on Lowland Peatlands in England and Wales (Policy Briefing Note WP2, Defra Project SP1218, March 2021)**  
  URL: https://lowlandpeat.ceh.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-07/Policy-brief-note-WP2.pdf  
  Key finding: Contemporary subsidence on remaining deep drained arable peat in the UK averages **1–2 cm/yr** (**0.03–0.25 cm/yr** from wind erosion); drained Fens farmland spans **50%** of Cambridgeshire and **40%** of Lincolnshire below sea level—supporting **80,000** jobs, **£3 billion/yr** regionally, and a third of England's fresh vegetables—while requiring **£3.5 million** in Department for Transport road repairs in Cambridgeshire alone and contributing to up to **10 Mt CO2e/yr** (**~3%** of 2019 UK GHG emissions) from English peatlands.

## 2. California Central Valley & Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta

- **San Joaquin Valley is Still Sinking — NASA Earth Observatory (2017)**  
  URL: https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/89761/san-joaquin-valley-is-still-sinking  
  Key finding: Excessive groundwater pumping since the **1920s** has caused up to **8.5 meters (28 feet)** of cumulative subsidence in sections of the San Joaquin Valley and an estimated **$100 million** in state/federal agency repairs since the **1960s**; NASA JPL InSAR analyses for California DWR documented drought-period rates up to **0.6 meters (2 feet) per year** near Chowchilla and Corcoran, plus up to **51 centimeters (20 inches)** over an **11-kilometer (7-mile)** area near Tranquility between **May 2015 and September 2016**.

- **Land Subsidence Along the California Aqueduct in West-Central San Joaquin Valley, California, 2003–10 — USGS Scientific Investigations Report 2018-5144 (Sneed, Brandt, & Solt, 2018)**  
  URL: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20185144  
  Key finding: Aquifer-system compaction from **1926 to 1970** locally exceeded **8.5 meters**, and renewed groundwater pumping during drought/surface-water reductions (**2007–2010**) lowered elevation along a **145-kilometer** reach of the California Aqueduct, compressing deposits primarily beneath the Corcoran Clay and reducing aqueduct freeboard and flow capacity.

- **Delta Subsidence in California: The Sinking Heart of the State — USGS Fact Sheet 005-00 (Ingebritsen, Ikehara, Galloway, & Jones, 2000)**  
  URL: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs00500 (PDF: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2000/fs00500/pdf/fs00500.pdf)  
  Key finding: Across the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta's **1,150-square-mile** area, **57** islands/tracts protected by **more than 1,100 miles** of levees experience long-term peat-oxidation subsidence of **1–3 inches per year**, leaving central Delta islands **10 to nearly 25 feet** below sea level, contributing to **about 100 levee failures** since the early **1890s**, and threatening freshwater exports (up to **7.5 million acre-feet/yr**) supplying **more than 20 million** Californians.

- **Friant-Kern Canal Slows By 60 Percent, Subsidence To Blame — KVPR / Valley Public Radio (Ezra David Romero, August 3, 2017)**  
  URL: https://www.kvpr.org/environment/2017-08-03/friant-kern-canal-slows-by-60-percent-subsidence-to-blame  
  Key finding: Citing the Friant Water Authority (Doug DeFlitch), a **25-mile** reach of the **152-mile** Friant-Kern Canal in Tulare County—built in the **1940s** to irrigate **1 million acres** and supply towns including Lindsey, Orange Cove, and **Terra Bella**—sank **two to three feet**, cutting flow capacity by about **60 percent** (from **4,000 cfs** design capacity down to **1,600 cfs**), with the **deepest sinking near Terra Bella** and estimated full-canal adjustment costs around **$400 million**.

- **Neely et al. (2021), *Water Resources Research* 57(4):e2020WR028451 (PMC8047915)**  
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8047915/  
  Key finding: Sentinel-1 InSAR and cGPS (`Oct 2015 – Sep 2017`) across the San Joaquin Valley report dry `WY2016` mean subsidence of **-4.20 cm/yr** (`-42.0 mm/yr`) and peak **-34.5 cm/yr** (`-345 mm/yr`), wet `WY2017` mean **-2.04 cm/yr** and peak **-17.7 cm/yr**, and `Apr 2015–Oct 2017` peak **-27.0 cm/yr**.

- **Ojha, Werth & Shirzaei (2019), *JGR Solid Earth* 124:3127–3143 (PMC6559157)**  
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6559157/  
  Key finding: Multi-sensor ALOS and Sentinel-1 InSAR, GPS, and GRACE inversions (`2007–2009` and `Jan 2015–Sep 2016`) document subsidence up to **-25.0 cm/yr** (`250 mm/yr`) in the Tule/Kaweah subbasins (`~ -15.0 cm/yr` near Chowchilla) and **~ -30.0** to **> -30.0 cm/yr** in the southern Central Valley.

## 3. Karapınar / Konya Basin Sinkholes (*Obruks*) & Subsidence

- **Konya İlinde Güncel Obruk Oluşumları (Current Sinkholes Formations in Konya Province) — Tapur & Bozyiğit (2015), *Marmara Coğrafya Dergisi* 31:415–446**  
  URL: https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/3454  
  Key finding: Inventories **104** sinkholes across Konya Province (**61** historical and **43** recent between **1972 and 2014**; **3** water-bearing, **40** dry, and **8** filled by landowners) triggered by karstic rock dissolution along groundwater flow toward Tuz Gölü and accelerated groundwater drawdown; provides field-measured GPS coordinates and dimensions for deep Karapınar obruks including **Akviran Obruğu** (**80 m** deep, **24 m** diameter, **1977**), **Yarımoğlu Obruğu** (**76 m** deep, **28 m** diameter, **2009**), **Nebili Obruğu-II** (**70 m** deep, **1972**), **Yavşançukuru Obruğu** (**56 m** deep, **21 m** diameter, **2000**), and **İnoba Obruğu** (**42 m** deep, **29 m** diameter, **2008**, prompting **7** families to relocate).  
  *(Used as the verified coordinate and morphometry source for all 29 features in `data/karapinar/sinkholes.geojson`.)*

- **Konya İli Obruklarının Turizm Potansiyeli (The Tourism Potential of Sinkholes in Konya Province) — Tapur & Bozyiğit (2016), *Marmara Coğrafya Dergisi* 34:253–267**  
  URL: https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/227372  
  Key finding: Notes **86** of Konya's **104** documented sinkholes are located in Karapınar district, documents recent **30-year** groundwater declines reflected in karstic lake drops, and measures iconic historical obruk lakes including **Kızören Obruğu** (**341 × 277 m** rim, **145 m** depth in 1992 reduced by **10 m**, designated a **127-hectare** Ramsar site in **2005**), **Meyil Obruğu** (**660 × 590 m**, **40 m** water depth), and **Çıralı Obruğu** (**354 × 303 m**).

- **Orhan et al. (2021), *Sensors* 21(3):774 (PMC7865528)**  
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7865528/  
  Key finding: Sentinel-1 (`2014–2018`) and COSMO-SkyMed (`2016–2017`) SBAS-InSAR paired with cGNSS across Karapınar shows subsidence exceeding **-7.0 cm/yr** (`> 70 mm/yr`) across agricultural bowls with **> 80%** cross-correlation to groundwater decline.

- **Üstün, Tuşat & Yalvaç (2010), *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences* 10:1151–1164**  
  URL: https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/10/1151/2010/  
  Key finding: Continuous and campaign GNSS monitoring (`2006–2009`) measures **-1.2 to -5.2 cm/yr** (`-12 to -52 mm/yr`) vertical land subsidence across the Konya Closed Basin.

- **Canaslan Çomut et al. (2015), *ISPRS Archives* XL-1/W5:115–119**  
  URL: https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-1-W5/115/2015/  
  Key finding: Multi-band InSAR (`ERS-1/2`, `Envisat`, `ALOS L-band`, `1992–2010`) documents **-1.3 to -1.7 cm/yr** (`-13 to -17 mm/yr`) regional multi-decadal subsidence trends in the Konya Basin.

## 4. UK Clay Shrink–Swell Geohazard (BGS)

- **Swelling and Shrinking Soils — British Geological Survey (BGS)**  
  URL: https://www.bgs.ac.uk/geology-projects/shallow-geohazards/clay-shrink-swell/  
  Key finding: Shrink–swell clays represent Britain's most damaging geohazard, costing the UK economy **£540 million** following the **1991** drought, while the Association of British Insurers (ABI) recorded over **10,000** household subsidence claims worth **£64 million** in **July–September 2018** (a **350%** quarterly surge—the highest quarterly increase in over **25 years** of records).

- **BGS GeoSure: Shrink–Swell — British Geological Survey (BGS)**  
  URL: https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/bgs-geosure-shrink-swell/  
  Key finding: Maps shrink–swell susceptibility across **Great Britain** at **1:50,000** scale, noting that shrink–swell ground movements—costing the British economy an estimated **£3 billion over the past decade**—occur predominantly in the **upper two metres** of soil where clay minerals swell when wet and shrink during dry conditions.

## 5. NISAR Mission, Data Products & Calibration Status

- **NISAR Mission Page — NASA Science**  
  URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/  
  Key finding: The NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar mission launched on **July 30, 2025**, operating dual-frequency **L-band (24 cm wavelength)** and **S-band (9.4 cm wavelength)** SAR instruments; L-band data are openly archived at NASA's Alaska Satellite Facility (ASF) DAAC, with **Provisional** L-band products first released on **July 20, 2026**.

- **NISAR Data Products Overview — ASF NISAR Data User Guide**  
  URL: https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/products-overview/  
  Key finding: Separates ASF-distributed NISAR L-band products into **BETA** (**January/February 2026**, not fully calibrated), **PROVISIONAL** (**July 2026** release covering **June 17, 2026 through part of October 23, 2026**, calibrated and partially validated), and **VALIDATED** (**November 6, 2026** release covering acquisitions from **October 21, 2026 onward**, fully calibrated and validated).

- **Pre-Calibration (BETA) Known Issues — ASF NISAR Data User Guide**  
  URL: https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/product-known-issues/  
  Key finding: Documents that pre-calibration BETA collections exhibit valid-data mask misalignment, cross-track antenna-pattern radiometric banding, and cross-track/along-track streaks in low-backscatter regions.

- **Provisional Product Known Issues — ASF NISAR Data User Guide**  
  URL: https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/provisional-known-issues/  
  Key finding: Details known caveats of **CRID P05023** PROVISIONAL data, including high-latitude ionospheric phase residuals/geolocation errors, radio-frequency interference (RFI) filter under/over-suppression, low-level SLC edge ripples, and residual Frequency B (**5 MHz**) radiometric errors.

- **Geocoded Unwrapped Interferogram (GUNW) — ASF NISAR Data User Guide**  
  URL: https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gunw  
  Key finding: L2 GUNW products are generated on Frequency A with **80 m** posting for unwrapped phase (radians), coherence magnitude, connected components mask, and split-spectrum ionospheric phase screen (included in the HDF5 file alongside solid Earth tide and tropospheric lookup tables, **not applied by default** to unwrapped phase).

## 7. Challenge Resources & Learn More (`NASA NISAR StoryMap`, `Applications White Papers`, `Portals & Training`)

Every resource from the NASA Space Apps *"Dancing with the SARs"* Resources tab below was opened and verified (`HTTP 200`):

- **NISAR ArcGIS StoryMaps — Land Subsidence (Peacock, A., An, K., Pinto, N., & Lou, Y., 2024; Zenodo `doi:10.5281/zenodo.10818833`)**  
  URL: https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/109e602e748346f3be8dc89124619828  
  Key finding: Frames groundwater pumping subsidence as an invisible global infrastructure risk led by **California's Central Valley and California Aqueduct** (airborne L-band **UAVSAR** measured a subsidence bowl exceeding **40 inches** during **2013–2017**, citing Miller et al. 2020 and Jones et al. 2020) and **Mexico City**, establishing the historical benchmark our 2026 spaceborne NISAR analysis updates.

- **NISAR Societal Benefits & Applications White Papers Portal — NASA Science / JPL**  
  URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/societal-benefits/ (JPL redirect: https://nisar.jpl.nasa.gov/applications/societal-benefit/)  
  Key finding: Organizes domain white papers explaining how systematic 12-day L-band and S-band SAR products translate surface deformation, backscatter, and polarimetry into operational water, hazard, and food-security monitoring.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Subsidence (`NISAR_Applications_Subsidence.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISAR_Applications_Subsidence.pdf  
  Key finding: Explains why gradual subsidence caused by subsurface compaction often goes unnoticed until canals, railways, and levees suffer structural grade loss, requiring routine satellite radar early warning.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Drought and Groundwater Withdrawal (`NISAR_Applications_Drought_and_Groundwater_Withdrawal1.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISAR_Applications_Drought_and_Groundwater_Withdrawal1.pdf  
  Key finding: Details the hydrological chain linking surface-water scarcity during drought to intensified aquifer pumping, irreversible fine-grained clay compaction, and infrastructure deformation.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Levees and Dams (`NISAR_Applications_Levees_and_Dams1.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISAR_Applications_Levees_and_Dams1.pdf  
  Key finding: Highlights how routine 12-day L-band interferometry supplements manual walk-over inspections across thousands of kilometres of earthen flood barriers by flagging localized settlement and seepage zones early.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Sinkholes and Cavern Collapse (`NISAR_Applications_Sinkholes1.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISAR_Applications_Sinkholes1.pdf  
  Key finding: Explains karst dissolution and subsurface cavern roof instability in carbonate bedrock (such as limestone), showing how SAR maps regional drawdown bowls and subtle precursor deformation around sinkhole zones.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Food Security & Crop Area Monitoring (`NISAR_Applications_Food_Security1.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISAR_Applications_Food_Security1.pdf  
  Key finding: Demonstrates how biweekly dual-polarization L-band backscatter (`HH` and `HV`) penetrates cloud cover to classify active cropland area and seasonal phenological growth.

- **NISAR Applications White Paper: Soil Moisture & Water Resources (`NISARApplications_SoilMoisture1.pdf`)**  
  URL: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/nisar/nisar-jpl/pdf/NISARApplications_SoilMoisture1.pdf  
  Key finding: Explains microwave radar sensitivity to soil dielectric constant changes driven by water content, enabling 12-day field-scale moisture retrievals across agricultural regions.

- **ASF NISAR Data User Guide (Documentation Portal)**  
  URL: https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/
